## 1. Strategy in one page

1. **Don't chase head terms yet.** "Artificial jewellery", "earrings online" and
   "fashion jewelry" are held by Amazon, Myntra, Meesho, Nykaa, Flipkart and
   established brands (Kushal's, Zaveri Pearls, Rubans, Voylla, Sukkhi). Put
   those terms on the homepage and category pages, but don't expect traffic
   from them in year one.
2. **Win with specific commercial long-tail keywords**, built as
   *style × product type × modifier* (e.g. "oxidised jhumkas for saree",
   "AD choker set under 1000"). Most of your traffic will come from these.
3. **Build the site structure before launch.** Every category,
   sub-category and collection page targets one primary keyword. No two
   pages target the same keyword.
4. **Use one English site for both markets.** Reach the diaspora through an
   "Indian Jewelry" hub plus explainer content, not a copy of the site per
   country (Section 3).
5. **Blog content supports the shop.** Styling, care and explainer
   posts capture early-stage searches and link down to category pages.
6. **Product feeds and visual platforms count as much as Google rankings.**
   Google Merchant Center (free Shopping listings and AI Overviews product
   panels), Pinterest and Instagram bring jewellery buyers sooner than
   organic rankings will.

## 3. India vs international: language and URL decision

### 3.1 The two markets search differently

| Concept | India phrasing | International / diaspora phrasing |
|---|---|---|
| The category | artificial jewellery, imitation jewellery, fashion jewellery | **Indian** jewelry, Indian jewellery (UK), Indian costume jewelry, desi jewelry |
| Spelling | jewellery | jewelry (US, Canada) / jewellery (UK, Australia) |
| CZ stones | AD jewellery, American diamond | CZ jewelry, cubic zirconia Indian set |
| Bridal | bridal jewellery set, dulhan set | Indian bridal jewelry, Indian wedding jewelry, Pakistani bridal jewelry |
| Earrings | jhumka, jhumki, chandbali | jhumka earrings, Indian earrings, Bollywood earrings |
| Location intent | online, cash on delivery, under ₹999 | Indian jewelry USA / UK / Canada, free shipping, near me (diaspora stores) |
| Explainers | rarely searched | what is a maang tikka, how to wear a nath, what to wear to an Indian wedding |

Also expect Hindi / Hinglish searches in India: *payal, kada, chudi, nath,
jhumki, haar, rani haar, mang tika*. Use them as secondary keywords and in
product titles where they fit naturally.

### 3.2 URL decision

**Recommendation: one English site, India-first, plus an `/indian-jewelry/`
hub for international buyers.** Don't create `/us/` and `/uk/` copies yet.

Why: Google clusters near-duplicate pages before it reads hreflang. Country
pages that differ only in currency usually get folded into one version, and
large amounts of thin duplicate pages can hurt the whole site. Only create
locale versions you can make genuinely different.

Move to subfolders (`/us/`, `/uk/` with self-referencing canonicals,
reciprocal hreflang, `x-default` on `/`, valid codes like `en-GB` not
`en-UK`) **only when** international orders are significant **and** each
locale has real differences: local pricing, shipping and returns, sizing,
local reviews and copy.

Until then:
- Show currency switching and international shipping clearly on product and
  hub pages.
- Use "jewellery" site-wide and "jewelry" on the international hub and the
  diaspora-focused posts.

### 5.6 Product pages

Product pages target very specific variants. Title formula:

> **[Finish/Material] + [Style] + [Product type] + [Key detail/inclusion]**

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use |
|---|---|
| Rani Set 04 | Gold-Plated Kundan Choker Necklace Set with Earrings & Maang Tikka |
| Earring Blue | Oxidised Silver-Tone Jhumka Earrings with Blue Stones |
| AD Set | Rose-Gold American Diamond (CZ) Necklace Set for Wedding |

Rules:
- Always write **"gold-plated" / "gold-tone"**, never just "gold". Not doing so attracts the
  wrong searches, causes returns and damages trust.
- Write your own descriptions. Never use the supplier's copy.
- Include in each description: dimensions, weight (lightweight is a selling
  point), plating type, stone type, closure, skin-safety (nickel/lead-free if
  true), care, occasion and the outfits it pairs with.

## 6. Long-tail keyword patterns

Pattern for generating candidates (validate each with keyword tools
before building anything):

```
[style]       × [product]        × [modifier]
kundan          jhumka              for saree / for lehenga
oxidised        choker set          under 500 / under 1000
AD / CZ         bangles             for wedding / for haldi
temple          necklace set        lightweight / daily wear
pearl           maang tikka         for bride / for bridesmaid
meenakari       chandbali           online / with price
antique gold    long haar           with earrings / combo
```

Build a page for a combination only if **all three** are true:
1. Keyword tools show real demand (any non-zero, consistent volume).
2. The search results show other **shop/collection** pages, not just blog posts
   (this confirms commercial intent).
3. You have enough distinct products to fill it (8+).

Otherwise, cover the combination as a **section** of the parent page
(a heading + short paragraph + filtered product row), not as a separate URL.

**Writing standard for each post** (from `content-strategy` + `ai-seo`):
- Put the target keyword in the H1, URL, first paragraph and at least one H2.
- Answer the main question in the **first 40–60 words**, so the answer can be
  lifted directly by Google snippets and AI assistants.
- Use comparison **tables** for "vs" posts and numbered steps for "how to" posts.
- Use real product photos styled on people. No stock images.
- Show the author's name, "Last updated" date and an FAQ block at the end.
- Link to 2–4 relevant category/collection pages with descriptive anchor text.
- No keyword stuffing.

**Calendar mix** (`content-strategy` 60/30/10, weighted toward search at launch):
~70% searchable (table above) · ~20% shareable (lookbooks, styling reels turned

## 10. Structured data and AI search

| Item | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Product` + `Offer` schema | Price, currency, availability, SKU, brand, images, shipping, return policy | Rich results; product data for Google Shopping and AI answers |
| `AggregateRating` / `Review` | Once real reviews exist. Never fabricate them | Star ratings in results; trust signal |
| `BreadcrumbList` | All category, collection and product pages | Clear hierarchy for Google |
| `FAQPage` | FAQ blocks on categories and guides | Answers that AI assistants can extract (Google limits FAQ rich results, but the content still helps) |
| `Organization` | Logo, social profiles, contact | Helps Google and AI systems recognise the brand |
| `Article` | Blog posts with author and dates | Freshness and authorship signals |
| Google Merchant Center | Submit the product feed at launch (free listings) with accurate titles, prices, GTIN/MPN or `identifier_exists=false`, colour and material | Gets you into Shopping, Images and AI Overviews product panels without needing rankings |
| robots.txt | Allow Googlebot, Bingbot, GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended | AI assistants can't recommend pages they can't crawl |
| Rendering | Product name, price and description must appear in the HTML without JavaScript | Crawlers and AI agents need to read the page |

**Presence beyond your own site** (`ai-seo` Pillar 3). AI assistants lean on
third-party mentions:
- **Pinterest:** a board per category and occasion, with pins linking to collection
  pages. Jewellery is one of Pinterest's strongest shopping categories.
- **YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels:** styling and "how to wear" videos with
  keyword-rich titles, descriptions and captions (AI systems read the
  text around the video, not the video itself).
- **Reviews:** Google Business Profile (if you have a physical address),
  Trustpilot or Judge.me, and marketplace reviews if you also sell on
  Amazon, Meesho or Etsy.
- **Etsy (for international buyers):** a useful test channel for which
  "Indian jewelry" terms convert abroad before you invest in locale pages.

## 11. Seasonal calendar

Pages need time to get indexed and rank. Publish (or refresh) each seasonal
page **8–10 weeks before** demand peaks. Keep seasonal URLs **permanent**
(e.g. `/occasion/diwali/`, not `/diwali-2026/`) and update them each year so
they build authority.

| Demand peak | Occasion | Publish/refresh by | Market |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nov–Feb | Wedding season (India) | Early Sep | IN + INTL |
| Feb | Valentine's Day | Early Dec | IN + INTL |
| Mar / Apr (moves yearly) | Eid | 8–10 weeks prior | IN + INTL |
| Apr–May | Akshaya Tritiya, summer weddings | Feb | IN |
| May | Mother's Day | Mar | IN + INTL |
| May–Aug | UK / US diaspora wedding season | Mar | INTL |
| Jul–Aug | Teej, Raksha Bandhan | May–Jun | IN |
| Sep–Oct | Navratri / Garba | Jul | IN + INTL |
| Oct–Nov | Karwa Chauth, Diwali | Jul–Aug | IN + INTL |

> **Right now (late Oct 2026):** a new site won't rank for this year's Diwali or
> wedding-season searches. Use Instagram, Pinterest and Merchant Center
> listings for this season. Publish the wedding, haldi, mehendi and
> wedding-guest pages anyway, so they're indexed and established by next year.

## 13. Validation worksheet (do this before building)

Put every keyword from Sections 5–8 into a sheet with these columns:

| Keyword | Volume (IN) | Volume (US/UK/CA) | Difficulty | Intent (T/C/I) | SERP type (shop / blog / mixed) | Target URL | Pillar | Priority | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Process:
1. **Expand** each primary keyword in Keyword Planner / Ahrefs / Semrush (set location
   to India, then US, UK and Canada separately).
2. **Check the search results by hand** for the top 50 keywords. If results are mostly
   marketplaces with no independent shops, lower the priority. If you see
   smaller D2C brands ranking, raise it.
3. **Assign** each keyword to exactly one URL. Merge or drop pages with no
   validated demand.
4. **Re-score** the blog list with real volume and difficulty numbers.
