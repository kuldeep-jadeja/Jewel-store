# India-First Next.js Jewellery Business Plan

**Status:** Working plan; keyword phrases are candidates, not validated search demand.

## 1. Confirmed starting point

- Product: artificial / fashion jewellery.
- First market: India.
- Stock: limited.
- Design direction: traditional, Rajputana- and darbari-inspired.
- Main use case: wedding wear, with everyday wear as a secondary use case.
- Current product groups: bangles, earrings, and necklace sets.
- Intended retail price: most items below ₹1,000; occasional items may be higher. This is a product-price range, not a confirmed average order value.
- Store direction: Next.js.

The positioning below is a draft to validate against the actual products and buyers. “Inspired” describes the design direction; do not claim a specific historic origin or traditional craft unless it can be substantiated.

## 2. Recommended business focus

Lead with **Rajputana- and darbari-inspired artificial jewellery for weddings and occasions**. Keep everyday pieces as a smaller supporting range if the stock has a distinct, lighter selection. This gives the store a recognizable point of view while still using all three product groups.

Organize inventory by both product type and occasion:

- Wedding and occasion: necklace sets, earrings, and bangles that belong to the same styling story.
- Everyday: only items genuinely suited to repeat daily wear; do not relabel heavy occasion pieces as everyday products.

Before listing an item, record its SKU, available quantity, landed cost, dimensions, weight, material/finish, components included, supplier, quality-check result, and photographs. State exactly what comes in a set. Avoid unsupported claims such as “hypoallergenic,” “nickel-free,” “waterproof,” or “anti-tarnish.”

Do not widen the assortment until orders and customer questions show which styles deserve depth. Treat limited stock as a reason to test product-market fit, not as a reason to create many thin collection pages.

### Keyword validation workflow

1. Make a keyword sheet from the table and add actual product names, components, colours, and outfit occasions from the stock list.
2. Check India-targeted keyword estimates in Google Keyword Planner or a tool export, recording location, language, date, and whether the figure is a range or exact estimate.
3. Review live India search results for the most important candidates. Note whether results are marketplaces, specialist stores, product listings, or guides; match the page type to the observed intent.
4. Assign one main intent to each URL. Merge overlapping phrases; do not build a page just because a phrase sounds attractive.
5. After launch, use Search Console query impressions and clicks to replace assumptions with observed data.

No volume, difficulty score, or ranking timeline is asserted in this plan.

## 4. SEO and Next.js launch requirements

Prioritize indexable, useful collection and product pages over a large blog. For launch, prepare:

- Home page, wedding/occasion collection, only the category pages supported by stock, product detail pages, About, Contact, Shipping, Returns, Privacy, and Terms pages.
- Server-rendered or statically generated product and collection content with stable URLs, unique titles/descriptions, canonical URLs, sitemap, robots rules, breadcrumb links, and descriptive internal links.
- Accurate product structured data for price, currency, availability, and the exact offer. Keep stock status consistent between the page, checkout, and any product feed.
- Product photos that show front, back, scale, details, and how the piece looks worn; descriptive alt text; mobile-first image sizes; and visible dimensions, weight, finish, components, care, dispatch estimate, and return terms.

Next.js is the storefront framework, not the complete commerce operation. Decide checkout and stock handling before implementation. A manual order flow can reduce initial integration work but requires careful stock reconciliation and customer follow-up. Automated checkout requires a commerce backend, payment integration, order state handling, and tested cancellation/refund flows. Never process or store card credentials directly in the custom app.

## 5. Launch and learning sequence

### Stage A: Validate the offer

Audit stock and costs, choose the hero wedding edit, and compare it with a smaller everyday edit. Ask likely buyers what occasion they shop for, what information they need before buying, and what makes them hesitate. Use their answers to refine the assortment and product copy.

### Stage B: Prepare the store

Set selling prices from landed product cost plus packaging, fulfilment, payment charges, expected returns/cancellations, tax treatment, and marketing allowance. Since many individual items are below ₹1,000, model whether shipping and payment costs leave enough contribution on a single-item order. Consider complementary product bundles only when the combined margin works; do not rely on discounts to make low-value orders viable. Confirm applicable India tax, consumer, invoice, and product-disclosure obligations with a qualified adviser. Test every order path and stock adjustment before inviting customers.

### Stage C: Soft launch and measure

Launch to a small, relevant audience. Track product views, add-to-cart or enquiries, paid orders, contribution after variable costs, sell-through by SKU, cancellations, returns, and order source. Use this evidence to decide what to restock and which page or channel needs improvement. Do not scale paid acquisition based on followers or traffic alone.

### Stage D: Expand only after repeatability

Add depth to proven styles when supplier quality and replenishment are reliable. Add more SEO content only when it answers questions real buyers ask or supports a collection that can convert. Keep international sales out of the launch scope; revisit after domestic operations, customer support, and margins are repeatable.
