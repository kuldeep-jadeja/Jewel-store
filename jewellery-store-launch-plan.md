# India-First Jewellery Store Launch Plan

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026  
**Status:** Working plan; business inputs marked “to decide” are intentionally left open.  
**Companion document:** [Jewellery keyword strategy](./jewellery-keyword-strategy.md)

## 1. Executive plan

Build a small, trustworthy online jewellery store for customers in India. Use the stock already available to test which products, styles, prices, and messages earn paid orders. Protect cash and customer trust while learning. Reorder and widen the range only when sales, margins, supplier quality, and fulfillment support it.

The first launch should have:

- A defined customer and one clear product angle.
- A carefully counted, photographed, quality-checked live assortment.
- A mobile-friendly store with honest product details, transparent shipping and return terms, and working payments.
- Domestic fulfillment that can meet the delivery promise shown to customers.
- A simple measurement sheet for order contribution, conversion, returns, COD failures, and sell-through.
- A small set of launch marketing channels; SEO and international expansion grow after product demand is proven.

This plan assumes the store sells artificial or imitation jewellery, as described in the existing keyword strategy. Confirm that product type before building the assortment or copy.

## 3. Strategy and stage gates

### The first objective

Prove that a focused product range can generate orders with positive contribution after variable costs and manageable customer-service work. Do not use follower growth, page views, or keyword rankings alone as proof that the business works.

### Gate A: Ready to open

Open the store when:

- Every listed product has a verified quantity, selling price, landed cost, and supplier record.
- Product claims, photos, and delivery promises match the real item and process.
- Payments, shipping rates, order notifications, refunds, stock updates, and customer contact paths have been checked end to end.
- The applicable tax, disclosure, and consumer-policy questions have been reviewed with a qualified Indian accountant or adviser.

### Gate B: Ready to spend more on acquisition

Increase paid promotion or influencer spend only after the team can estimate contribution per delivered order and track the source of each order. Set an affordable customer-acquisition ceiling from the margin model; do not choose an ad budget first and hope sales make it work.

### Gate C: Ready to expand the assortment

Add depth when customer orders and questions identify a specific winner, the supplier can replenish it reliably, quality checks pass, and the expected contribution remains positive.

### Gate D: Ready to explore international sales

Consider a one-country pilot only after domestic delivery, refunds, customer support, and replenishment are repeatable. Before the pilot, model shipping, duties, customs documentation, payment settlement, local returns, delivery times, and country-specific consumer requirements.

## 4. Pick the launch position

Choose one primary customer and a narrow reason to buy. Possible directions to evaluate include everyday lightweight pieces, oxidised designs, festive styling, or wedding guest jewellery. These are candidate positions, not decisions.

Write a one-sentence positioning statement:

> For [specific customer] who wants [occasion or need], [brand] offers [distinctive product benefit] with [proof customers can verify].

Proof could be accurate weight and dimensions, clear close-up photos, a documented plating description, reliable packaging, a genuine limited drop, or a useful styling service. Avoid unsupported claims such as “hypoallergenic,” “nickel-free,” “handcrafted,” or “premium plated.”

Before committing to a position:

1. Review five relevant Indian stores or marketplace listings. Note prices, product descriptions, delivery promises, return terms, photo quality, and the questions buyers ask.
2. Speak with a small group of likely buyers. Ask what they buy, what makes them hesitate, what information they need, and what price feels reasonable. Do not treat likes as willingness to pay.
3. Compare answers with the stock on hand. Select the position the stock can credibly deliver.

## 5. Inventory, sourcing, and product quality

Create a master SKU sheet before uploading products. Use one row per sellable variant.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| SKU and product name | Order accuracy and stock reconciliation |
| Style, occasion, colour, variant | Navigation and collection building |
| Quantity available | Prevent overselling |
| Supplier and purchase date | Traceability and reordering |
| Purchase cost and inbound freight | Landed-cost calculation |
| Material and finish details | Accurate customer expectations |
| Measurements and weight | Fit, comfort, and expectation setting |
| QC result and defects | Returns prevention |
| Supplier lead time and minimum reorder | Replenishment planning |
| Photo and listing status | Launch readiness |

For the limited stock, inspect every unit before listing. Check stone setting, clasp and closure, sharp edges, plating consistency, pair matching, colour against the product photo, and packaging condition. Record damaged or inconsistent pieces and do not sell them as standard stock.

Set an inventory rule:

- Store quantity in one system of record and update it as soon as an order is accepted.
- Use an inventory warning at the reorder point based on supplier lead time and the stock buffer you choose.
- When an item sells out, mark it unavailable promptly. Offer a restock notification only if you can collect consent and give a realistic update.
- Avoid taking preorders unless you can state a dependable dispatch date and have a process for delays and refunds.
- Do not create indexed SEO pages for empty, unavailable, or nearly duplicate assortments.

## 6. Pricing and unit economics

Calculate a contribution estimate for every SKU and for a typical order. Use actual supplier, courier, gateway, and packaging quotes.

**Contribution before marketing per delivered order**

= net order sales, excluding any applicable tax collected  
− landed product cost  
− packaging and pick/pack cost  
− shipping subsidy  
− payment or COD charges  
− expected cost of cancellations, RTO, refunds, and return handling  
− other variable order costs

**Contribution after marketing**

= contribution before marketing − acquisition cost for that order

Use this model to decide:

- Which products can absorb a discount or shipping offer.
- Whether COD is available for all orders, selected PIN codes, or selected customers.
- Whether bundles make sense and what minimum order value, if any, supports free shipping.
- The maximum acquisition cost the business can afford.
- Which products should be reordered, repriced, bundled, or removed.

Keep GST treatment separate from sales revenue and confirm the correct invoicing treatment with an accountant. Do not set a free-shipping threshold until the order economics support it.

## 7. Store and customer experience

### Platform selection

Compare hosted store platforms and self-managed options against these requirements. Choose after confirming the budget and who will maintain the store.

- Indian payment support, including the payment methods you actually intend to offer.
- COD controls, courier integration, serviceability by PIN code, tracking, and return workflows.
- Inventory updates that prevent overselling across the channels you use.
- GST invoice configuration and accounting exports.
- Mobile speed, product variants, SEO controls, data export, and support.
- Total costs: subscription or hosting, themes, apps/plugins, payment fees, courier fees, and maintenance.

A managed platform may reduce technical upkeep. A self-managed platform can offer more control when you already have the skills or support to maintain it. Do not select based on “SEO-friendly” claims alone.

### Minimum useful site structure

Start with:

- Home page explaining the brand and showing the current assortment.
- Shop or collection pages only for cohesive stock you can fulfill.
- Individual product pages for every sellable product.
- About, contact, order tracking, shipping, returns/refunds, privacy, and terms pages.
- A visible customer-support method and grievance contact.
- Clear cart and checkout with delivery estimate and shipping cost before payment.

Keep the navigation simple. A buyer should reach a product quickly from mobile and understand the price, delivery, and return terms before checkout.

### Product-page checklist

For every product:

- Use a descriptive name that identifies style, item type, finish, and key inclusion.
- Show the actual item in clear close-ups and a worn or scale photo where practical.
- State dimensions, weight when useful, material/finish, closure, what is included, care, and known limitations.
- Say “gold-plated” or “gold-tone” only when it accurately describes the item; do not imply solid gold.
- Make availability, dispatch time, delivery estimate, shipping fee, and return eligibility easy to find.
- Use original supplier-independent descriptions and photos.
- Ask for reviews only after a real purchase; never invent ratings or testimonials.

## 8. Operations and fulfillment

Write a one-page order procedure before launch:

1. Receive the paid or COD order.
2. Confirm payment status, item, variant, address, and available stock.
3. For COD, use a proportionate confirmation step to reduce avoidable failed deliveries.
4. Pick and inspect the item; pack it to prevent tangling, crushing, moisture, or scratching.
5. Dispatch by the stated cutoff and send tracking information.
6. Reconcile the order, inventory, payment, courier status, and any COD remittance.
7. Handle delivery issues, exchanges, refunds, and complaints using the published policy.
8. Record the reason for every return, cancellation, defect, or failed delivery.

Choose a courier based on coverage, tracking, pickup reliability, reverse logistics, serviceability, and total cost—not just its lowest headline rate. Start with areas where the delivery promise is dependable. Maintain a simple daily queue for paid, COD, packed, dispatched, delivered, return, and refund statuses.

## 9. India-specific compliance and trust checks

Treat these as pre-launch work with an accountant or qualified adviser; rules can depend on the business structure, state, product packaging, sales channel, and current law.

- **GST and invoicing:** CBIC’s FAQ distinguishes an example of own-account sales through a seller’s own website from marketplace supplies collected by an operator. It says the own-site example does not require TCS on the seller’s own products, while the supply remains subject to applicable GST. Confirm current registration, tax, invoice, and state/interstate obligations for your exact setup with a CA. [CBIC sectoral FAQs](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/sectoral-faq.html)
- **Ecommerce consumer disclosures:** Review the current Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules and amendments for the information, grievance, cancellation, refund, and other obligations that apply to your store. [Department of Consumer Affairs: Consumer Protection](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/acts-and-rules/consumer-protection/consumer-protection)
- **Packaged-commodity declarations:** Ask whether your jewellery packaging falls under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules and what declarations must appear on the package and online listing. The Department describes mandatory declarations for covered pre-packaged commodities. [Legal Metrology overview](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/organisation-and-units/division/legal-metrology/overview) · [Packaged Commodities Rules](https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/legalmetrologyactsandrules/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-2011)
- **Privacy and payments:** Publish a privacy notice, limit access to customer data, collect only what is needed for orders and support, and use an established payment provider. Confirm current data-protection and payment obligations with an adviser.
- **Claims and promotions:** Keep descriptions, photos, discounts, delivery promises, and product claims accurate. State sale terms and exclusions clearly.

## 10. Marketing and demand validation

### Launch channel mix

Keep the first mix manageable:

1. **Owned store:** The canonical destination for product details, checkout, policies, analytics, and customer service.
2. **Visual social channel:** Publish real product photos, try-on videos, styling demonstrations, packaging, and restock updates. Use links that identify the source of visits.
3. **Customer messaging:** Use an opt-in list for order support, care instructions, and relevant restock announcements. Avoid unsolicited promotional messages.
4. **Google surfaces:** Set up Search Console and Merchant Center after product pages, shipping, and returns information are accurate. Google free listings are available in India, but eligibility does not guarantee that products will be shown. India is among the countries where Merchant Center requires shipping settings or shipping cost in product data. [Google Merchant Center: Free listings](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434?hl=en)
5. **Small creator or paid tests:** Try only after calculating the affordable acquisition cost and using tracked links or codes. Scale the creative and audience that produce delivered orders with acceptable contribution.

### Content plan

Use the existing keyword strategy as a backlog, not as a launch checklist.

At launch, create only content that helps shoppers choose among products actually in stock. Start with:

- A concise brand and materials explanation.
- A care guide that matches the products sold.
- One useful styling guide tied to the core collection.
- A short comparison or FAQ addressing questions customers actually ask.

Add more pages when Search Console, support conversations, and customer interviews reveal demand. The original 24-post calendar can be rescored later. Seasonal pages should be prepared only when the store has stock, delivery capacity, and a current offer for that occasion.

### Search and product visibility setup

- Verify the store in Search Console and submit its sitemap.
- Configure analytics for product views, add-to-cart, checkout, purchase, refunds, and campaign sources.
- Keep product price, availability, shipping, and return information consistent across the site and Merchant Center.
- Use accurate product structured data and never fabricate AggregateRating or Review data.
- Keep faceted filters and parameter URLs from multiplying indexable pages.
- Track product approvals and policy issues in Merchant Center each week at launch.

## 11. Relative launch schedule

Treat this as a planning sequence, not a fixed promise. Begin the next phase only when its inputs are ready.

| Phase | Suggested timing | Main work | Output / gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Decide and audit | Weeks 1–2 | Select customer and product angle; count stock; confirm suppliers, costs, lead times, and quality; talk to likely buyers | Positioning sentence, SKU sheet, initial contribution model |
| 2. Prepare operations | Weeks 2–3 | Choose platform and payment/courier setup; set service area, dispatch cadence, COD approach, and return process; review compliance with advisers | Policies, order workflow, launch area |
| 3. Build the storefront | Weeks 3–5 | Create core pages, product listings, original photos, inventory controls, analytics, Search Console, and Merchant Center | Store ready for end-to-end checkout check |
| 4. Test and soft launch | Weeks 5–6 | Test paid and COD orders, stock changes, notifications, packing, tracking, cancellations, and refunds; invite a small first audience | Issues fixed; first customer feedback |
| 5. Learn and adjust | Days 1–30 after launch | Review orders and customer questions weekly; fix listing and checkout friction; monitor stock and delivery | First sales and service baseline |
| 6. Replenish selectively | Days 31–60 | Compare products by delivered contribution, returns, and restock reliability; reorder only clear candidates | Reorder decision tied to evidence |
| 7. Scale or revise | Days 61–90 | Review channel economics and operating load; expand validated collections or change the position | Written continue / revise / pause decision |
| 8. Consider overseas pilot | After domestic proof | Model one destination country, taxes, duties, delivery, payment, returns, and support | Go/no-go for a tightly scoped pilot |

## 12. Weekly operating scorecard

Record values by week and by product where practical.

| Measure | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Available stock and sell-through by SKU | Which items sell and how quickly stock is consumed |
| Visits by source and product-page conversion | Whether the audience and product page are working |
| Add-to-cart and checkout completion | Where shoppers abandon the purchase |
| Orders, average order value, and net sales | Sales volume and basket size |
| Contribution before and after marketing | Whether orders add cash after variable costs |
| COD confirmation, delivery, and RTO rate | Whether COD is commercially workable |
| Returns, refunds, defects, and reason codes | Product and expectation gaps |
| Dispatch time and delivery exceptions | Whether fulfillment matches the promise |
| Repeat customers and opt-in restock interest | Early retention and product demand |
| Merchant Center approvals and Search Console clicks | Product-feed and organic-search health |

Avoid setting arbitrary “success” thresholds before you know your price, margin, stock quantity, and operating capacity. Use the first launch period to establish a baseline, then set thresholds that protect contribution and customer experience.

## 13. Main risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Overselling scarce stock | Single inventory source, prompt stock updates, tested checkout quantity controls |
| Product quality varies by batch | Inspect each unit; keep supplier and batch records; pause listings with inconsistent stock |
| Buyers misunderstand finish or size | Accurate terms, dimensions, worn photos, care guidance, no exaggerated material claims |
| COD orders are refused | Track delivery outcomes; use confirmation steps; review COD eligibility and fees |
| Returns erase margin | Include expected return/RTO costs in SKU economics; make policy and product facts clear |
| Too many empty or thin pages | Publish only useful pages supported by current stock and validated search intent |
| Paid traffic is unprofitable | Set CAC ceiling from contribution; test small and track delivered sales |
| International expansion distracts | Keep the first catalog and fulfillment domestic; use a formal expansion gate |
